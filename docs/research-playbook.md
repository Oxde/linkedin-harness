# Research and engagement playbook

## Research modes

### A. News or source-led post

1. Fetch the original source first.
2. Save actor, event, date, numbers, quote, caveat.
3. Find the weirdest verified detail and the social or business tension.
4. Draft from the event, not from a generic moral.

### B. Product or repository launch

1. Inspect the product, repo, docs or demo.
2. Name the dumb workflow it removes.
3. Show only the three or four details that prove it works.
4. Use a product screenshot or demo as the visual where possible.

### C. LinkedIn intelligence scan

Purpose: learn what works in the lane and find posts worth engaging with.

Read-only inputs:

- the owner's recent posts and their numbers;
- search results for a small set of lane terms;
- a deliberate list of 10–20 relevant accounts, refreshed monthly.

For each candidate record: author and URL, visible engagement and timing, the hook, the proof or
visual, the emotion or stake, why it worked, and an angle the owner could take. Never copy text.

Return 5–10 candidates, recommend at most three to engage with, and draft comments only after
the user selects.

### D. Account learning

Use the last 20–30 posts when numbers are available. Rank by:

- engagement rate = (reactions + comments + reposts) / impressions
- comment ratio = comments / reactions
- reach multiple = impressions / followers
- saves and sends where visible

Compare top and bottom posts on hook, image, length, topic, opinion timing and first-hour
replies. Posting day and time are the last explanation.

## Comments on other people's posts

Pick one:

- add a real datum
- add the missing case
- disagree, respectfully
- extend the strongest line
- ask the hard specific question
- share a short receipt from experience
- correct a fact, briefly and kindly
- reframe the idea
- a one-liner only when it is genuinely funny or true

Never restate the post or write "great post". Drafts go to `templates/COMMENT_QUEUE.md`.

## Replies under the owner's posts

Triage in this order:

- **lead** — a real problem or a possible buyer
- **substance** — useful data, a question, a disagreement, a correction
- **peer** — a high-signal person worth a real exchange
- **support** — simple praise or a tag
- **noise** — spam, pitches, bad faith

Answer public questions publicly. Every external action needs the user's approval of the exact
text.
