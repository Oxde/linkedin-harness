# Hook patterns

Line one is the whole post for most readers. On a phone, LinkedIn shows about two lines before
"see more", so line one names the subject and line two pays it off.

Every pattern below is a prior, not evidence. The owner's own top posts (see `linkedin-audit`)
beat this list; add their best openers to `VOICE.md` and prefer those.

Rules for every hook:

- A named thing (company, product, person, repo, place) **and** a number, a behaviour or a pain.
- It must be true without the rest of the post. No hook that the body walks back.
- No hype words, no emoji sirens, no question the post never answers.
- Write three from different patterns; keep the one with the most specific noun.

The examples are invented to show the shape. Never ship an example's number.

| # | pattern | shape | example | use when | how it fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Number + claim** | `<thing> did <specific result>.` | "One config file cut our agent bill from $31 to $3." | the source has a hard number | the number is rounded up or not sourced |
| 2 | **The weird verified detail** | the strangest true fact, flat | "The model passed the test by deleting the test." | news with one odd detail | the detail is a rumour, or it is not in the source |
| 3 | **Claim check** | `<claim people repeat>. Is it true?` then line 2 answers | "Small models now match the big ones on agent tasks. Mostly true." | a launch with a big claim | line 2 dodges the answer |
| 4 | **The cost** | what it cost, in money or hours | "We lost two days to one missing environment variable." | a field note or failure | no lesson follows the cost |
| 5 | **Free beats paid** | `<free thing> does what <paid thing> charges for.` | "A free repo now does what we paid $400 a month for." | a tool or skill post | the free one needs a paid account to work |
| 6 | **Tried N, kept K** | `I tried N <things>. I kept K.` | "I tried 30 agent skills this month. I kept 4." | a ranked list from real use | N or K is made up |
| 7 | **The behaviour** | one thing it does, said as a fact | "This skill refuses to write code until a test fails." | a skill or repo breakdown | the behaviour is a README promise nobody ran |
| 8 | **The scene** | one line from a real moment | "The agent opened the PR at 3am. It was empty." | a story with a turn | the scene is invented |
| 9 | **The rule** | a decision rule the reader can use today | "If a task fits in one context window, don't split it into agents." | a guide or opinion | the rule has no reason in the body |
| 10 | **Before → after** | two states, one change | "Monday: 6 tools. Friday: 1 script." | a workflow change | the "after" is not measured |
| 11 | **The disagreement** | names the common view, then where it breaks | "Everyone says post at 8am. Our best post went out at 11pm." | an opinion with evidence | it is contrarian with no data |
| 12 | **Who should care** | names the reader and the stake | "If you run Claude Code on a team, this changes your review step." | a niche tool or change | the group is too broad to feel named |

## The second line

Line two decides the "see more" tap. It does one of three jobs:

- **pays off** line one in plain words (what it is, who it is for);
- **twists** it ("Not because X.", "Mostly true.", "The catch is Y.");
- **raises the stake** (the number that makes line one matter).

It never restates line one or starts a setup that pays off three paragraphs later.

## Check

- Read only lines 1–2. Does a stranger know the subject and want line 3?
- Is every number in them from the evidence ledger, with a source and a date?
- Would the owner say this out loud to a friend? If not, it is not their hook.
